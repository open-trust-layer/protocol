Open Layer Protocol exists to make trust portable and verifiable
without making trust centrally owned.

1. Evidence over reputation.
2. Facts over judgments.
3. Standardize evidence, not judgment.
4. Participant-owned history.
5. Contextual trust.
6. No universal trust score.
7. Algorithm plurality.
8. Privacy by architecture.
9. Minimum disclosure by default.
10. Identity != trust.
11. Actor neutrality.
12. No silent history rewriting.
13. No authority without provenance.
14. Blockchain neutrality.
15. Jurisdiction neutrality.
16. Interoperability before invention.
17. Independent verifiability.
18. Small, conservative core.

These principles are normative design constraints for Open Layer Protocol.
Features and protocol changes should be rejected when they materially violate them.
